Malaysia Economic Inequality Dashboard

Analyzing income distribution, poverty rates, and regional disparities across Malaysian states

About This Dashboard

This interactive dashboard provides a comprehensive view of economic inequality in Malaysia, tracking trends from 1970 to 2022. It examines how income and wealth are distributed across different states, identifies regions with the highest poverty rates, and shows the relationship between household income and expenditure.

The data reveals significant regional disparities, with states like Sabah and Kelantan facing much higher poverty rates compared to more developed regions like Kuala Lumpur and Selangor.

National Gini Coefficient Trend (1970-2022)
Line Chart
Tracks Malaysia's income inequality over five decades. The Gini coefficient ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality). Malaysia has made significant progress, reducing inequality from 0.557 in 1976 to 0.404 in 2022.
Household Income Inequality by State (2022)
Choropleth Map
Shows Gini coefficients across Malaysian states. Darker colors indicate higher income inequality within the state. Sabah has the highest inequality (0.395), while Pahang has the lowest (0.308).
Absolute Poverty Rates by State (2022)
Bar Chart
Displays the percentage of population living below the poverty line in each state. Sabah has the highest poverty rate at 19.7%, while Putrajaya has the lowest at 0.1%.
Mean Household Income by State (2022)
Lollipop Chart
Compares average household income across states. Putrajaya and Kuala Lumpur have the highest incomes (over RM 13,000), while Kelantan has the lowest (RM 4,885).
Household Income vs Expenditure by State (2022)
Bubble Chart
Examines the relationship between household income and spending patterns. Bubble size represents poverty rate. Higher income generally correlates with higher expenditure, but some states show different spending patterns relative to their income levels.

Key Insights

Income Inequality Has Improved: Malaysia's Gini coefficient has decreased from 0.557 in 1976 to 0.404 in 2022, showing significant progress in reducing income inequality over five decades.
Regional Disparities Are Significant: States like Sabah (19.7%) and Kelantan (13.2%) have poverty rates more than 10 times higher than Kuala Lumpur (1.4%) and Putrajaya (0.1%).
Income and Poverty Correlation: States with higher mean household incomes generally have lower poverty rates, though the relationship isn't perfect due to varying costs of living and economic structures.
Urban-Rural Divide: Federal territories and more urbanized states (Kuala Lumpur, Putrajaya, Selangor) show significantly better economic outcomes than predominantly rural states.
Eastern Malaysia Challenges: Both Sabah and Sarawak continue to face substantial economic challenges compared to peninsular states, with higher poverty rates and lower average incomes.
