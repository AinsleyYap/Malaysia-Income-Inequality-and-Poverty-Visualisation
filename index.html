Malaysia's Income Inequality & Poverty

Analyzing income distribution, poverty rates, and regional disparities across Malaysia

National Overview: Malaysia's Income Inequality Landscape

Malaysia's National Income Inequality Trend (1970-2022)

Tracking Malaysia's income inequality over five decades, the Gini coefficient ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality). Malaysia has made significant progress, reducing inequality from 0.557 in 1976 to 0.404 in 2022.
Malaysia shows consistent progress in reducing inequality over five decades, demonstrating structural economic transformation. This long-term trend reflects systematic efforts through education, industrialization, and social policies to improve wealth distribution.

State-Level Analysis: Regional Economic Disparities

How Income Inequality, Poverty, and Household Income Interconnect

  • Distinct Regional Economic Patterns: Clear income hierarchies emerge across Malaysia, with wealth concentrated in federal territories and coastal urban centers, while eastern and rural states face persistent inequality and poverty.
  • Interlinked Inequality, Poverty, and Income Levels: States with higher inequality often experience elevated poverty rates and lower household incomes, revealing how uneven economic growth limits overall prosperity.

Household Income Inequality by State (2022)

The Gini coefficients across Malaysian states. Darker colors indicate higher income inequality within the state. Sabah has the highest inequality (0.395), while Pahang has the lowest (0.308).
Eastern Malaysian states show higher internal inequality despite lower overall development, indicating concentrated economic benefits. Wealthier states like Selangor and Kuala Lumpur prove that economic growth and equitable distribution can coexist with effective policies.

Absolute Poverty Rates by State (2022)

This visualisation shows the percentage of population living below the poverty line in each state. Sabah has the highest poverty rate at 19.7%, while Putrajaya has the lowest at 0.1%.
The data reveals extreme regional disparities in Malaysia's poverty landscape. While federal territories and developed states have largely conquered absolute poverty, Eastern Malaysia faces challenges comparable to some developing nations. What's particularly telling is that Sabah's poverty rate (19.7%) exceeds the national average of many developing countries, while Putrajaya's (0.1%) rivals developed nations. This internal divide represents one of Malaysia's most pressing development challenges.

Mean Household Income by State (2022)

Comparing the average household income across states, Putrajaya and Kuala Lumpur have the highest incomes (over RM 13,000), while Kelantan has the lowest (RM 4,885).
Malaysia shows extreme regional income inequality, with federal territories prospering while Eastern and Northern states face substantial economic challenges. The gap is staggering: Kelantan's income (RM 4,885) is less than 40% of Putrajaya's (RM 13,473), representing one of Southeast Asia's largest regional divides.

From Patterns to Behavior: Analyzing Spending Decisions

Household Income vs Expenditure by State (2022)

This visualisation examines the relationship between household income and spending patterns along side with poverty rates represented by bubble size.
This visualization reveals that income alone doesn't determine spending patterns across Malaysian states. The most telling insight is the spending-pressure paradox in Eastern Malaysia, where households in Sabah and Sarawak spend relatively more of their income despite lower earnings, highlighting regional cost disparities and economic challenges.
