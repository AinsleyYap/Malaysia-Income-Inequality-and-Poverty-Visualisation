Malaysia: Income Inequality & Poverty Across States

Analyzing income distribution, poverty rates, and regional disparities across Malaysian states

This dashboard explores economic inequality across Malaysia to better understand regional disparities and temporal trends. We begin by examining the national picture of income inequality over time, then dive into state-level analysis to explore variations in income distribution, identify areas with the highest poverty rates, and analyze household spending patterns relative to income.

Malaysia's National Income Inequality Trend (1970-2022)

Tracking Malaysia's income inequality over five decades, the Gini coefficient ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality). Malaysia has made significant progress, reducing inequality from 0.557 in 1976 to 0.404 in 2022.
Malaysia shows consistent progress in reducing inequality over five decades, demonstrating structural economic transformation. This long-term trend reflects systematic efforts through education, industrialization, and social policies to improve wealth distribution.

Key Insights & Observations

Remarkable Progress

Malaysia reduced income inequality by 27.5% since its peak in 1976, showing sustained commitment to economic reforms.

Policy Impact Visible

The steep decline from 1976-1990 aligns with the New Economic Policy implementation, demonstrating policy effectiveness.

Recent Stabilization

Since 2014, inequality has stabilized around 0.40, suggesting Malaysia has reached a new equilibrium in income distribution.

Historical Context

The 1976 peak (0.557) represents one of Southeast Asia's highest inequality levels, making subsequent progress even more significant.

Household Income Inequality by State (2022)

The Gini coefficients across Malaysian states. Darker colors indicate higher income inequality within the state. Sabah has the highest inequality (0.395), while Pahang has the lowest (0.308).
Eastern Malaysian states show higher internal inequality despite lower overall development, indicating concentrated economic benefits. Wealthier states like Selangor and Kuala Lumpur prove that economic growth and equitable distribution can coexist with effective policies.

Regional Inequality Insights

Eastern Malaysia Challenge

Sabah (0.395) has the highest inequality, reflecting the urban-rural divide and economic disparities within the state despite natural resource wealth.

Pahang's Relative Equality

With the lowest Gini coefficient (0.308), Pahang shows more balanced income distribution, potentially due to diversified economic activities.

Urban Centers Moderate

Kuala Lumpur (0.380) and Selangor (0.361) show moderate inequality despite high incomes, suggesting effective urban poverty alleviation programs.

Regional Patterns

Eastern states (Sabah, Sarawak) generally show higher inequality than peninsular states, highlighting the East-West development gap.

Absolute Poverty Rates by State (2022)

This visualisation shows the percentage of population living below the poverty line in each state. Sabah has the highest poverty rate at 19.7%, while Putrajaya has the lowest at 0.1%.
The data reveals extreme regional disparities in Malaysia's poverty landscape. While federal territories and developed states have largely conquered absolute poverty, Eastern Malaysia faces challenges comparable to some developing nations. What's particularly telling is that Sabah's poverty rate (19.7%) exceeds the national average of many developing countries, while Putrajaya's (0.1%) rivals developed nations. This internal divide represents one of Malaysia's most pressing development challenges.

Poverty Landscape Insights

Extreme Disparities

Sabah's poverty rate (19.7%) is 197 times higher than Putrajaya's (0.1%), highlighting severe regional development gaps.

Critical Intervention Zones

Three states exceed 10% poverty: Sabah (19.7%), Kelantan (13.2%), and Sarawak (10.8%) - requiring urgent targeted policies.

Success Stories

Federal territories show near-zero poverty: Putrajaya (0.1%), Kuala Lumpur (1.4%), and Labuan (2.5%) - models for poverty eradication.

Middle Tier States

Most peninsular states cluster between 4-9% poverty, showing consistent but incomplete progress in poverty reduction.

Mean Household Income by State (2022)

Comparing the average household income across states, Putrajaya and Kuala Lumpur have the highest incomes (over RM 13,000), while Kelantan has the lowest (RM 4,885).
Malaysia shows extreme regional income inequality, with federal territories prospering while Eastern and Northern states face substantial economic challenges. The gap is staggering: Kelantan's income (RM 4,885) is less than 40% of Putrajaya's (RM 13,473), representing one of Southeast Asia's largest regional divides.

Income Distribution Insights

Federal Territory Advantage

Putrajaya (RM 13,473) and Kuala Lumpur (RM 13,325) lead by significant margins, benefiting from administrative and economic centrality.

Eastern Malaysia Gap

Sabah (RM 6,171) and Sarawak (RM 6,457) lag behind, earning roughly half of what federal territories make, highlighting development disparities.

Industrial Corridor Effect

Selangor (RM 12,233) and Johor (RM 8,517) show strong performance, benefiting from industrialization and cross-border economic activities.

Income Multiplier

The highest income (Putrajaya) is 2.76 times the lowest (Kelantan), showing significant economic stratification across states.

Household Income vs Expenditure by State (2022)

This visualisation examines the relationship between household income and spending patterns along side with poverty rates represented by bubble size.
This visualization reveals that income alone doesn't determine spending patterns across Malaysian states. The most telling insight is the spending-pressure paradox in Eastern Malaysia, where households in Sabah and Sarawak spend relatively more of their income despite lower earnings, highlighting regional cost disparities and economic challenges.

Spending Behavior Insights

Strong Income-Expenditure Correlation

Wealthier states like Putrajaya and Kuala Lumpur show proportional spending, indicating sustainable consumption patterns aligned with income levels.

Eastern States' Spending Pressure

Sabah and Sarawak show relatively higher expenditure relative to income, suggesting cost-of-living pressures despite lower incomes.

Savings Potential Variations

States like Selangor and Johor show healthy income-expenditure gaps, indicating better savings capacity and financial resilience.

Poverty-Spending Paradox

Some high-poverty states maintain moderate expenditure levels, revealing complex household budgeting strategies in challenging economic conditions.
